# Wholesale Regulations Knowledge Base

## 50 State Review of Wholesaling Regulations

This document provides findings from a comprehensive 50-state review of regulations governing the marketing and sale of wholesaled properties. For states not listed, no specific regulations were identified that would cause compliance issues.

## State-by-State Regulations

### Alabama
**Disclosure Requirements:**
Wholesaler must disclose in writing:
1. **To Potential Purchaser:** The nature of the buyer's equitable interest
2. **To Seller:**
   - Buyer's intent to market the equitable interest prior to marketing
   - Effective date of the assignment at least 3 days prior to the effective date

### Arizona
**Pre-Contract Disclosures:**
Prior to entering into a binding contract, wholesaler must disclose:
1. **To Seller:** That the buyer is a wholesale buyer
2. **To Subsequent Buyer:**
   - That the seller is a wholesale seller holding an equitable interest
   - That the seller may not be able to convey title to the property

### California
**License Requirement:**
- **Broker license required** if a person, for another or others:
  - Sells or offers to sell
  - Buys or offers to buy
  - Exchanges or offers to exchange a real property sales contract

### Florida
**Unclear Status:**
- Sale of a contract to purchase real estate is NOT included in broker activity definition
- However, definition of real property includes an assignment
- **Further research required** to clarify intent

### Illinois
**License Required - Pattern of Business:**
- Broker definition includes entities that engage in a **pattern of business** of:
  - Buying, selling, offering to buy or sell
  - Marketing for sale
  - Exchanging or dealing in contracts (including assignable contracts)
- **"Pattern of business"** means:
  - 2 or more occurrences in any 12-month period
  - Whether alone or with other individuals/entities as partners or common owners

### Kentucky
**License Required:**
- Real estate brokerage includes:
  - Advertising for sale an equitable interest in a contract to purchase real property
  - Between owner and prospective purchaser

### Maryland
**License Required - Frequency Based:**
- Real estate brokerage services include:
  - Dealing in contracts, including assignable contracts
  - On **2 or more occasions** in any 12-month period

### Nebraska
**License Required:**
- Real estate broker includes:
  - Marketing for sale an equitable interest in a contract for purchase of real property
- **NRCE requires** an assignable contract addendum to be signed

### North Dakota
**License Required with Specific Disclosures:**
- Real estate broker includes:
  - Marketing for sale an equitable interest in a contract for purchase of real property
- **Written disclosures required** to all parties:
  1. Wholesaler holds an equitable interest in the property
  2. May not be able to convey title to the property
  3. Intends to make a profit or income from the transfer of the equitable interest

### Oklahoma
**License Required:**
- Broker license required to:
  - Publicly market or sell an equitable interest
  - In a contract for the purchase of real property

### Pennsylvania
**State Level:**
- Wholesaling/sale of contract to purchase is NOT included in real estate brokerage definition
- No state license required

**Philadelphia (City-Specific):**
- Requires **Residential Property Wholesaler License** if:
  - Person/entity is in the business of purchasing
  - Soliciting for purchase residential properties
  - Not to use as their residence

### South Carolina
**License Required - Commission Interpretation:**
- **Statutory exemption** for unlicensed owner of real estate
  - Applies only if interest being sold is identical to owner's legal interest
- **Commission interpretation:**
  - Equitable interest ≠ Owner's legal interest
  - Individual with only equitable interest is NOT the property owner
  - Must have real estate brokerage license
- **Additional requirements:**
  - Licensees must have listing agreement with legal owner before marketing
  - Cease and Desist Orders issued for unlicensed practice

### Texas
**License Not Required BUT Disclosures Mandatory:**
- **No license required** IF properly disclosed
- **Failure to disclose** = considered engaged in real estate brokerage = license required

**Required Written Disclosures** BEFORE entering into contract:
1. **To Potential Buyer:**
   - Person is selling only an option or assigning interest in a contract
   - Person does NOT have legal title to the real property
2. **To Property Owner:**
   - Person intends to sell an option or assign interest in a contract

### Washington
**License Required - Department Guidance:**
- **2010 DOL guidance** states wholesaling may require broker license under:
  - Chapters 18.85, 18.86.030, and 18.235 RCW
- **When license required:**
  - Person obtains interest in real property from owner
  - With intent of finding another buyer before completing transaction
  - Person is "putting transactions together for others"
- **DOL action:** Will consider taking action against perpetrators for unlicensed practice

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Q: What if a wholesaler operates in multiple states?**
A: Must comply with each state's requirements where properties are located. Licenses may be required for each state.

**Q: Are online activities subject to these rules?**
A: Yes, online marketing and advertising are considered "public marketing" in most states.

**Q: What about "equitable interest" vs "legal interest"?**
A: Most states distinguish between equitable interest (what wholesalers have) and legal interest (what owners have). This distinction often determines license requirements.

**Q: How often can a wholesaler transact before needing a license?**
A: Varies by state:
- IL, KY, MD: 2+ transactions in 12 months
- Others: Any transaction may require license

**Q: What happens if caught operating without required license?**
A: Penalties can include:
- Cease and desist orders
- Fines
- Injunctions
- Criminal charges in some cases
- Civil liability

## Detailed State Regulations with Recent Updates

### Illinois
**Real Estate License Act of 2019** - First state to pass wholesale regulations:
- Requires active real estate broker's license for wholesalers
- Cannot offer real estate advice or conduct negotiations for clients
- Wholesaling violates "highest and best" credo
- Can do 1 wholesale transaction per 12-month period without license
- Must follow all real estate agent laws including income disclosure
- Illinois Attorney General can sue for violations with tens of thousands in penalties

### Nebraska
**LB892 (2022)**:
- Requires real estate license for "publicly marketing for sale an equitable interest"
- New Commission Policy lists agency and procedural requirements
- Agent assisting wholesaler is seller's agent for the wholesaler
- Requires Assignable Contract Addendum
- New Agency Disclosure form

### Oklahoma
**Predatory Real Estate Wholesaler Prohibition Act** (Effective Nov 1, 2021):
- Licensing requirement: Must obtain real estate license
- Marketing restrictions: Cannot publicly market unless holds title
- Disclosure obligations: Must disclose intent to assign contracts

**Senate Bill 1075** (Effective Nov 1, 2025):
- **Redefines Wholesaling**: Securing/negotiating/facilitating transaction with purpose of transferring/assigning/selling equitable interest for financial profit
- **Written Disclosure Requirements**:
  - Intent to wholesale for higher price
  - Seller should seek legal advice
  - Right to cancel within 2 business days
- **Rules**:
  - Cannot act as advisor or consultant
  - Cannot represent as licensed without certification
  - Cannot place lien or encumbrance
  - Must include wholesaler's name, number, address
  - Must include property address
  - Must include total consideration
  - Must include complete payment terms

### Arizona
**HB 2747 (2022)**:
- Requires written disclosure to seller that buyer is a wholesaler
- Seller may cancel contract without penalty and retain earnest money if disclosure violated

### Kentucky
**HB62 (2023)**:
- Redefines "real estate brokerage" to include advertising for sale an equitable interest
- Limits this advertising to licensed real estate brokers

### North Dakota
**HB 1190 (2023)**:
- Defines "wholesaler" and requirements
- Must provide written disclosures to all parties
- Provides recourse to seller if disclosure violated
- Provides recourse to buyer if disclosure violated

### Alabama
**SB 228 (2023)**:
- Requires wholesaling disclosure requirements
- Regulates long-term listing agreements (NTRAPS)
- Penalties: Class C misdemeanor, civil liability up to 3x fee received
- NTRAPS deemed unenforceable, $10,000 statutory damages

### Oregon
**HB 4058 (2024)**:
- Wholesalers must register with state (unless licensed)
- Registration requirements: High school diploma/GED, over 18, competency test, background check
- Written disclosure required
- Seller has 3 days to cancel if violation
- Penalties: Equal to wholesale profit, max 364 days imprisonment, $6,250 fine

### Indiana
**HB 1068 (2024)**:
- Defines "unlicensed real estate solicitor"
- Must disclose in all communications (print/digital) that not licensed
- Must include legal names
- Seller has 2 days to cancel if improper disclosure
- Exceptions for inquiries without offers or intent to take title

### Wisconsin
**AB 918 (2024)** - Effective Immediately:
- Does NOT require real estate license
- Defines wholesaling as assignment of contract method only
- Must disclose to seller as "real property wholesaler"
- Must disclose to buyer about assigning equitable interest
- Penalties: $5,000 fine and/or 6 months jail per offense

### Iowa
**HF 2394 (2024)** - Waiting for governor signature:
- Defines "wholesaler" as licensee or person represented by licensee
- Must be licensed as real estate broker to engage in wholesaling
- Must disclose legal identities, wholesaling process explanation
- Must provide agency agreement copy
- Civil penalty up to $10,000 or 10% of sale price

### Virginia
**HB 917 (Effective July 1, 2024)**:
- Adds to "real estate broker" definition: deals in real estate contracts on 2+ occasions in 12 months
- Requires real estate license

### Pennsylvania
**SB 1173** - Once governor signs, effective 180 days later (2025):
- Defines wholesaling
- Wholesaling is brokering, requires active PA RE license
- Must provide written disclosures
- Seller has unwaivable right to cancel (30 days with proper disclosure, anytime without)

### City of Philadelphia
**Residential Property Wholesaler License**:
- Must get registered
- Must provide property disclosures to sellers
- Apply: https://www.phila.gov/services/permits-violations-licenses/get-a-license/business-licenses/rental-and-property/get-a-residential-property-wholesaler-license/

### City of Atlanta
**Law prohibiting commercial harassment** (2020):
- Cannot contact seller more than once after they say not interested
- Subject to fines for violations

### Georgia
**SB90 (Effective Jan 1, 2024)** - Direct mail disclosure:
- Unsolicited agreements must be clearly marked as solicitations
- Required notices on envelope and in letters
- "THIS IS A SOLICITATION" in capital letters

### South Carolina
**HB 4754 (2024)** - Effective immediately:
- Marketing and selling property you don't own is brokering, requires license
- Licensees not allowed to wholesale
- "Wholesaling" does not include assigning contractual rights
- Distinguishes between marketing contractual position vs. underlying property

### Tennessee
**Case Law (Soto v. Presidential Properties, LLC., 2021)**:
- Selling equitable interest (assignment, double closing, novation) is brokering
- Requires active RE license

**SB 0990 (2025)** - Effective immediately:
- Defines wholesaling as selling "equitable interest" to new buyer for higher price
- Must disclose nature of equitable interest to both buyer and seller
- Must inform seller 3 days prior to contracting with new buyer

### Washington
**SB 1081 (2025)** - Effective Jan 1, 2026:
- Seller has 3 days to order appraisal at buyer's expense
- After appraisal, seller has 4 days to cancel without penalty
- If no appraisal, seller has 10 days to cancel
- Exceptions: Does not apply to publicly listed properties or if represented by WA licensed agent

### Rhode Island
**HB 5185 (2025)** - Effective immediately:

## Current Bills in Progress

### North Carolina
**HB 797 - We Buy Houses Homeowner Protection Act** (Introduced 4/07/25):
- Defines wholesaling to include soliciting, negotiating, marketing purchase contracts
- Requires real estate broker's license
- 30-day right to cancel for any reason
- 14-point font disclosure requirement
- Attorney general enforcement authorized

### West Virginia
**HB 3501 - The Abolishment of Wholesaling Act** (Introduced 3/18/25):
- Would formally prohibit wholesaling in the state
- Allows sellers/buyers to cancel contracts without penalty
- Can retain or receive refunds of earnest money

### Ohio
**Senate Bill 155** (Introduced March 26, 2025):
- Requires clear disclosure statement to property owners
- Encourages homeowners to seek legal advice
- Disclosure must be signed and dated before binding contract

### Connecticut
**House Bill 5572** (Introduced January 21, 2025):
- Establishes licensing and regulation requirements
- Sets standards for wholesale contracts
- Reported out of committee, on House calendar
