# Real Estate Market Analysis Guide

## Overview

Market analysis helps investors understand local real estate conditions and make informed decisions. This guide covers key metrics, data sources, and analysis techniques.

## Key Market Indicators

### Price Metrics

**Median Home Price**
- The middle price point of all homes sold
- Less affected by outliers than average
- Track month-over-month and year-over-year changes

**Price Per Square Foot**
- Useful for comparing properties of different sizes
- Varies significantly by neighborhood
- Calculate: Sale Price / Livable Square Feet

**List-to-Sale Price Ratio**
- Shows negotiating power in market
- Above 100% = seller's market (bidding wars)
- Below 95% = buyer's market (room to negotiate)

### Supply Metrics

**Months of Inventory**
- How long current supply would last at current sales pace
- Below 3 months = seller's market
- 3-6 months = balanced market
- Above 6 months = buyer's market

**Active Listings**
- Total properties currently for sale
- Compare to historical averages
- Seasonal adjustments needed

**New Listings**
- Fresh inventory entering market
- Indicates seller confidence
- Compare to pending/sold volume

### Demand Metrics

**Days on Market (DOM)**
- Average time from listing to contract
- Low DOM = high demand
- Compare to area historical average

**Absorption Rate**
- Percentage of inventory sold per month
- High rate = strong demand
- Calculate: Monthly Sales / Active Listings

**Pending Sales**
- Properties under contract
- Leading indicator of closed sales
- Shows near-term demand

### Rental Metrics

**Median Rent**
- Typical rent for the market
- Track by property type and bedroom count
- Compare to regional averages

**Vacancy Rate**
- Percentage of units unoccupied
- Below 5% = landlord's market
- Above 8% = renter's market

**Rent Growth**
- Year-over-year rent increases
- Strong markets: 3-5%+ annually
- Weak markets: flat or declining

## Economic Indicators

### Employment

**Job Growth Rate**
- Percentage change in employment
- Strong markets: 2%+ annual growth
- Correlates with housing demand

**Unemployment Rate**
- Compare to state and national averages
- Watch for trends over time
- Consider industry diversity

**Major Employers**
- Identify largest employers
- Watch for expansions or layoffs
- Diversification reduces risk

### Population

**Population Growth**
- Key driver of housing demand
- Track net migration trends
- Compare to regional averages

**Household Formation**
- New households = new housing demand
- Track age demographics
- Millennials driving current demand

### Income

**Median Household Income**
- Affects affordability and rent levels
- Track growth over time
- Compare to home prices

**Income-to-Price Ratio**
- Affordability indicator
- Higher ratio = more affordable
- Watch for divergence trends

## Analysis Framework

### Step 1: Market Overview

1. Define the market boundaries (city, MSA, zip codes)
2. Gather baseline metrics:
   - Median price and price trends
   - Inventory levels
   - Days on market
   - Population and job growth

### Step 2: Trend Analysis

1. Compare current metrics to:
   - 3 months ago
   - 12 months ago
   - 3-5 year averages
2. Identify acceleration or deceleration
3. Note any anomalies or outliers

### Step 3: Comparative Analysis

1. Compare to similar markets
2. Compare to state averages
3. Compare to national trends
4. Identify relative value

### Step 4: Submarket Analysis

1. Break down by neighborhoods
2. Identify hot spots and weak areas
3. Note price disparities
4. Assess gentrification trends

### Step 5: Risk Assessment

1. Identify economic vulnerabilities
2. Assess supply pipeline (new construction)
3. Evaluate regulatory risks
4. Consider natural disaster risks

## Market Cycle Phases

### 1. Recovery
- Vacancy declining
- Rent growth minimal
- Limited new construction
- Good time to buy

### 2. Expansion
- Strong rent growth
- Low vacancy
- New construction starting
- Peak buying opportunity passing

### 3. Hypersupply
- New construction surging
- Vacancy increasing
- Rent growth slowing
- Caution advised

### 4. Recession
- High vacancy
- Rents declining
- Construction halted
- Distressed opportunities

## Hot Market Indicators

Signs of a strong market:
- Population growth above 1.5% annually
- Job growth above 2% annually
- Days on market under 30
- Months of inventory under 3
- Price appreciation above 5% annually
- Multiple offers common
- Rising rents

## Warning Signs

Red flags in a market:
- Population decline
- Major employer leaving
- Rising inventory levels
- Increasing days on market
- Flat or declining prices
- New construction oversupply
- Rising vacancy rates
- Rent concessions appearing
