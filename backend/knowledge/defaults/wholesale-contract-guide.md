# Wholesale Real Estate Contract Guide

## Overview

Wholesale contracts are the legal foundation of real estate wholesaling. This guide covers the key elements and clauses needed to protect your interests.

## Essential Contract Components

### 1. Purchase and Sale Agreement

The primary contract between you (the wholesaler) and the seller. Key elements:

- **Parties**: Full legal names of buyer and seller
- **Property Description**: Full address and legal description
- **Purchase Price**: The agreed-upon price
- **Earnest Money Deposit (EMD)**: Typically $500-$5,000
- **Closing Date**: Usually 30-45 days out
- **Inspection Period**: 7-14 days for due diligence

### 2. Assignment Clause

**Critical for wholesaling!** This clause allows you to assign (transfer) your contract rights to an end buyer.

Sample language:
> "Buyer reserves the right to assign this contract to a third party without the consent of the Seller. Upon assignment, the original Buyer shall be released from all obligations under this contract."

### 3. Inspection/Due Diligence Clause

Allows you to cancel the contract if issues are found:

Sample language:
> "Buyer shall have [14] days from the Effective Date to conduct inspections and due diligence. Buyer may terminate this agreement for any reason during this period and receive a full refund of earnest money."

### 4. Exit Clauses (Contingencies)

Essential contingencies to include:

**Financing Contingency:**
> "This agreement is contingent upon Buyer obtaining financing satisfactory to Buyer within [30] days."

**Partner Approval:**
> "This agreement is contingent upon approval by Buyer's business partner(s) within [14] days."

**Title Contingency:**
> "This agreement is contingent upon Buyer receiving a satisfactory title report within [14] days."

## Assignment of Contract

When you find an end buyer, you'll use an Assignment Agreement.

### Key Assignment Elements

1. **Original Contract Reference**: Date and parties of original agreement
2. **Assignor**: You (the wholesaler)
3. **Assignee**: Your end buyer
4. **Assignment Fee**: Your wholesale profit
5. **Deposit from Assignee**: Typically non-refundable
6. **Terms Transfer**: All rights and obligations transfer

### Assignment Fee Structures

- **Disclosed Fee**: Assignee knows your profit (transparent)
- **Undisclosed**: Use double closing to hide margins
- **Partial Assignment**: Keep some interest in the deal

## Double Closing Alternative

When you don't want to disclose your profit or the assignment fee is large:

### Process:
1. Close with Seller (A-B transaction)
2. Immediately close with End Buyer (B-C transaction)
3. Use transactional funding or end buyer's funds

### When to Double Close:
- Assignment fee exceeds $15,000-$20,000
- Seller doesn't allow assignments
- Want to maintain buyer/seller privacy
- Complex multi-party deals

## Important Clauses to Include

### Access Clause
> "Seller grants Buyer and Buyer's agents reasonable access to the property for inspection, appraisal, and showings to potential partners."

### As-Is Clause
> "Buyer accepts the property in its current condition. Seller makes no warranties regarding the condition of the property."

### Earnest Money Protection
> "Earnest money shall be held by [Title Company Name] in escrow. In the event of cancellation within the inspection period, earnest money shall be returned to Buyer within [5] business days."

### Seller Disclosure
> "Seller agrees to disclose any known material defects, liens, encumbrances, or title issues affecting the property."

## Contract Checklist

### Before Signing:
- [ ] Property address verified
- [ ] Seller's legal name and authority confirmed
- [ ] Purchase price agreed upon
- [ ] Assignment clause included
- [ ] Inspection period adequate (14+ days)
- [ ] Earnest money amount reasonable
- [ ] Closing date allows time to find buyer
- [ ] Exit contingencies in place

### After Signing:
- [ ] Earnest money deposited with title company
- [ ] Title search ordered
- [ ] Property inspection scheduled
- [ ] Marketing to buyers list initiated
- [ ] Contractor walk-through scheduled

## Red Flags in Contracts

1. **No inspection period**: Always have an exit
2. **Short closing window**: Need 30+ days typically
3. **High earnest money**: Keep at reasonable levels
4. **No assignment clause**: Can't wholesale without it
5. **Unclear title**: Verify ownership before signing
6. **Seller financing terms unclear**: Get everything in writing

## State-Specific Considerations

Wholesale contract requirements vary by state:

- **Some states require disclosure** that you intend to assign
- **Licensing requirements** vary - check your state laws
- **Contract forms** may need to be state-approved
- **Earnest money handling** rules differ by state

**Always consult with a local real estate attorney** to ensure your contracts comply with state and local laws.

## Common Wholesale Exit Strategies

1. **Assignment**: Transfer contract to end buyer for fee
2. **Double Close**: Buy and immediately resell
3. **Novation**: Replace yourself with end buyer in contract
4. **Cancel**: Use contingencies to exit if no buyer found
