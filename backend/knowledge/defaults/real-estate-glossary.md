# Real Estate Investment Glossary

## Common Terms and Definitions

### A

**After Repair Value (ARV)**
The estimated value of a property after all repairs and renovations are completed. Used to calculate potential profit in fix-and-flip deals.

**Appraisal**
A professional assessment of a property's market value performed by a licensed appraiser.

**Assignment (of Contract)**
Transferring the rights and obligations of a purchase contract to another buyer, typically for a fee. Common in wholesaling.

**Assignment Fee**
The profit earned by a wholesaler when assigning a purchase contract to an end buyer.

### B

**BRRRR**
Buy, Rehab, Rent, Refinance, Repeat. An investment strategy for building a rental portfolio using forced appreciation.

**Buy Box**
A set of criteria defining an investor's ideal target property. Used to quickly filter and evaluate deals.

### C

**Cap Rate (Capitalization Rate)**
Net Operating Income divided by property value. Used to evaluate and compare income properties.
Formula: NOI / Property Value = Cap Rate

**Cash Flow**
The net income remaining after all expenses are paid. Calculated monthly or annually.

**Cash-on-Cash Return**
Annual cash flow divided by total cash invested. Measures return on actual cash invested.
Formula: Annual Cash Flow / Cash Invested = Cash-on-Cash Return

**Clear Title**
Ownership free of liens, encumbrances, or legal questions that could challenge ownership.

**Closing Costs**
Fees and expenses paid at the real estate transaction closing, typically 2-5% of purchase price.

**Comparative Market Analysis (CMA)**
An analysis of similar recently sold properties to estimate a property's value.

**Comps (Comparables)**
Recently sold properties similar to the subject property, used to determine market value.

**Contingency**
A condition that must be met for a contract to become binding, such as inspection or financing contingencies.

### D

**Days on Market (DOM)**
The number of days a property has been listed for sale before going under contract.

**Disposition**
The process of selling a property, particularly in wholesaling or flipping.

**Double Close (Simultaneous Close)**
Two closings happening on the same day - buying from seller and selling to end buyer - to avoid assignment disclosure.

**Due Diligence**
The investigation and verification process before completing a real estate transaction.

### E

**Earnest Money Deposit (EMD)**
A deposit made by a buyer to demonstrate serious intent to purchase. Typically held in escrow.

**Equity**
The difference between a property's market value and the outstanding mortgage balance.

**Escrow**
A neutral third party (often a title company) that holds funds and documents during a transaction.

### F

**Fair Market Value (FMV)**
The price a property would sell for on the open market between willing buyers and sellers.

**Fix and Flip**
An investment strategy of purchasing distressed properties, renovating them, and selling for profit.

**Forced Appreciation**
Increasing property value through improvements rather than market appreciation.

### G

**Gross Rent Multiplier (GRM)**
Property price divided by annual gross rental income. Quick way to evaluate rental property.
Formula: Purchase Price / Annual Rent = GRM

### H

**Hard Money Loan**
Short-term, asset-based loan from private lenders. Higher rates but faster funding than traditional loans.

**Holding Costs**
Ongoing expenses while owning a property: mortgage, taxes, insurance, utilities, maintenance.

**Hybrid Close**
Combination of assignment and double close, depending on deal specifics.

### I

**Inspection Period**
Time frame after contract signing during which buyer can inspect the property and potentially cancel.

### J

**Joint Venture (JV)**
Partnership between two or more parties to complete a real estate transaction, sharing resources and profits.

### L

**Leverage**
Using borrowed money to increase potential return on investment.

**Lien**
A legal claim against a property for unpaid debts, such as mortgages, taxes, or contractor bills.

**List Price**
The price at which a property is offered for sale.

### M

**Market Value**
The most probable price a property would bring in a competitive market.

**Maximum Allowable Offer (MAO)**
The highest price an investor can pay and still make their target profit.
Formula: ARV x 70% - Repairs = MAO

**MLS (Multiple Listing Service)**
A database of properties for sale, accessible to real estate agents and some investors.

**Motivated Seller**
A property owner who needs to sell quickly, often willing to accept below-market offers.

### N

**Net Operating Income (NOI)**
Gross rental income minus operating expenses (excluding mortgage payments).
Formula: Gross Income - Operating Expenses = NOI

### O

**Off-Market Deal**
A property for sale that is not listed on the MLS or publicly advertised.

**Owner Financing (Seller Financing)**
When the property seller provides financing to the buyer instead of a traditional lender.

### P

**Principal, Interest, Taxes, Insurance (PITI)**
The four components of a typical monthly mortgage payment.

**Private Money**
Loans from private individuals rather than institutional lenders.

**Pro Forma**
Projected financial performance of a property based on assumptions about income and expenses.

**Purchase Agreement**
A legal contract between buyer and seller outlining the terms of a real estate transaction.

### R

**Rehab (Rehabilitation)**
The renovation or repair of a property to increase its value.

**Rental Yield**
Annual rental income as a percentage of property value.

**Return on Investment (ROI)**
Total return divided by total investment, expressed as a percentage.

### S

**Seller's Market**
Market conditions where demand exceeds supply, giving sellers advantage.

**SFR (Single Family Residential)**
A standalone home designed for one family.

**Skip Trace**
The process of finding contact information for property owners.

**Subject-To (Sub-To)**
Purchasing a property subject to the existing mortgage, which remains in the seller's name.

**Sweat Equity**
Value added to a property through owner's labor rather than cash investment.

### T

**Title**
Legal ownership of a property.

**Title Insurance**
Insurance protecting against defects in title or claims against ownership.

**Title Search**
Examination of public records to verify ownership and identify liens or claims.

**Transactional Funding**
Short-term (often same-day) loans used for double closings in wholesale deals.

**Turnkey Property**
A fully renovated, often tenanted, investment property ready for immediate rental income.

### U

**Under Contract**
A property with an accepted offer but not yet closed.

**Underwriting**
The process of evaluating a loan application or investment opportunity.

### V

**Vacancy Rate**
Percentage of rental units that are unoccupied.

**Value-Add**
Properties with opportunity to increase value through improvements or better management.

### W

**Wholesale (Wholesaling)**
Finding properties under contract and assigning those contracts to end buyers for a fee.

**Wraparound Mortgage (Wrap)**
A financing arrangement where a new mortgage "wraps around" an existing one.

### Z

**Zestimate**
Zillow's automated estimate of a property's market value.

**Zoning**
Local regulations governing how property can be used (residential, commercial, etc.).

## Common Formulas Quick Reference

| Metric | Formula |
|--------|---------|
| MAO (70% Rule) | ARV × 0.70 - Repairs |
| Cap Rate | NOI / Property Value |
| Cash-on-Cash | Annual Cash Flow / Cash Invested |
| ROI | (Gain - Cost) / Cost × 100 |
| GRM | Price / Annual Gross Rent |
| NOI | Gross Income - Operating Expenses |
| Equity | Market Value - Loan Balance |
| 1% Rule | Monthly Rent ≥ 1% of Purchase Price |
| 50% Rule | Operating Expenses ≈ 50% of Gross Rent |
